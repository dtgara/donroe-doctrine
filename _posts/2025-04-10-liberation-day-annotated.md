On April 2, 2025 — designated Liberation Day by the White House — the United States announced reciprocal tariffs on imports from 185 countries and territories. The announcement was made in the Rose Garden. The President held up a chart. The chart's methodology was subsequently questioned by economists, trading partners, and at least one member of the President's own economic team.

The following is a record of the first ninety days.

**The announcement**

The tariffs were described as reciprocal. The formula used to calculate reciprocity divided each country's trade surplus with the United States by its total exports to the United States and halved the result. Several countries immediately noted that this formula does not describe reciprocity in any conventional sense. The administration maintained that it does.

The baseline tariff was 10 percent. Country-specific tariffs ranged upward from there. The European Union faced 20 percent. China faced 34 percent. Several small island nations with negligible trade volumes received tariff schedules that appeared to have been generated by the formula without human review.

**The pause**

On April 9, the administration announced a 90-day pause on country-specific tariffs above the 10 percent baseline. China was exempted from the pause. The President cited "the bond markets" as a factor in the decision. This was noted.

The pause was described by the President as a negotiating tactic that had already worked, evidenced by the willingness of 75 countries to contact the administration. No deals were announced. The 90-day clock began.

**The negotiations**

The UK reached a framework agreement in May. India remained in a standoff. The EU remained in a standoff. China's tariffs escalated to 145 percent, then were paused following the Geneva talks in May. The Geneva de-escalation reduced mutual tariffs to 30 and 10 percent respectively, pending further negotiation.

**The claim**

The President stated in April 2025: "The tariffs will come down very substantially once they make a deal with us. Everybody wants to make a deal." This statement is listed in the Prediction Tracker as pending. At time of writing, structural tariffs remain in place with the majority of trading partners. Several deals have been announced. None have been finalised in a form that substantially reduces the Liberation Day tariff structure.
