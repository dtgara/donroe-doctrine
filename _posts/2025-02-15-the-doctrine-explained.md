The Donroe Doctrine is not a doctrine in the traditional sense. It has no founding text, no formal articulation, and no consistent application. It is called a doctrine because something that recurs this reliably in foreign policy, across two terms separated by four years, deserves a name.

The core principle, insofar as one can be identified, is this: every international relationship is a transaction, every transaction has a price, and the price is set by the party willing to threaten more. Alliances are leverage. Agreements are opening positions. Multilateral institutions are optional at best and hostile at worst.

This is not an ideology in the conventional sense. It does not emerge from a coherent theory of international relations. It emerges from a consistent set of instincts applied to an inconsistent set of situations. The results are documented here.

**On the question of consistency**

The archive contains 53 documented contradictions as of February 2025. This figure is not offered as a criticism. It is offered as data. The doctrine contradicts itself in ways that are themselves patterned: adversaries are praised until they are denounced, allies are pressured until they comply, and compliance is treated as confirmation that pressure works, which generates more pressure.

The pattern is not one of deception. It is one of escalation followed by declaration of victory, followed by the next escalation. The cycle is the doctrine.

**On the question of effectiveness**

Several entries in the Deals archive document outcomes that were, by conventional measures, successful. The Abraham Accords normalised relations between Israel and four Arab states. The NATO 2 percent target — derided as unachievable — became standard, then was raised to 5 percent. The phase one China trade agreement, whatever its limitations, was signed.

Whether these outcomes were caused by the doctrine or occurred despite it is a question the archive does not attempt to resolve. The archive records what happened. Causation is left to the reader.

**On the purpose of this archive**

The Donroe Doctrine generates volume. Statements, contradictions, threats, deals, withdrawals, and predictions accumulate faster than any single reader can track. The archive exists to make the accumulation legible.

The register does not moralise. The facts, arranged in sequence, produce their own effect.
