The Toll counter on this site displays a number. The number increases continuously. This dispatch explains what the number represents, how it was derived, and what its limitations are.

**What was USAID**

The United States Agency for International Development disbursed approximately $43 billion annually in foreign assistance as of fiscal year 2024. Of this, roughly $8 billion supported global health programmes: HIV/AIDS treatment and prevention, malaria control, tuberculosis treatment, maternal and child health, and emergency food aid. The remainder funded democracy promotion, economic development, infrastructure, and disaster response.

On February 3, 2025, the President signed an executive order initiating the dissolution of USAID. Secretary of State Marco Rubio was designated Acting Administrator. By the end of February, the majority of USAID's 10,000 employees had been placed on administrative leave. Most active programme funding was frozen pending review. Most did not resume.

**The methodology**

The Toll counter uses an estimated mortality rate of 500,000 per year attributable to USAID health programme cessation. This figure is derived from pre-dissolution USAID impact assessments, peer-reviewed modelling of the PEPFAR and malaria programme contributions, and estimates published by the Lancet and by global health economists in the months following the dissolution.

The figure is a midpoint estimate. It does not include deaths attributable to the cessation of food aid programmes, the suspension of development assistance that affects longer-term mortality, or the indirect effects on health systems that relied on USAID institutional support. The actual figure is likely higher. It may be lower. The archive uses the published midpoint.

**What the counter does not show**

The counter does not show names. It does not show countries. It does not show the distribution of mortality across PEPFAR recipients, malaria-endemic regions, and maternal health programme beneficiaries. These figures exist in disaggregated form in public health literature. The counter shows only the aggregate.

The counter began on the date the programme funding ceased. It has not stopped.
