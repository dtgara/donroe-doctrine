Base case (50%): the Court of International Trade sets the 25-state challenge on a normal briefing schedule rather than granting immediate relief, on the theory that Section 301's investigation-backed origins are legally distinguishable from the emergency-powers claim the Supreme Court rejected in February. The tariffs continue collecting revenue toward USTR's own decade-long estimate while litigation proceeds, and the front-loaded cargo that defined July unwinds through August roughly on the Port Tracker's own schedule.

Downside case (30%): the states persuade the court that Section 301's investigation record — opened and closed within months, reaching findings against more than 80 countries simultaneously — reads as pretextual the same way the emergency-powers claim did, and CIT grants expedited relief, reopening the refund and customs-uncertainty period that defined the spring. Concurrently, the Fed's 9-3 split, with three regional presidents publicly dissenting toward a hike, signals that a further tariff-driven inflation surprise could force Chair Warsh's hand before the committee's own September 16 meeting.

Tail case (20%): the talks that followed Friday's paused strike produce a framework on Strait passage within the watch period, and Brent's own 24 percent July gain reverses hard enough that falling energy costs offset the tariff litigation risk entirely — handing the administration a rare instance of two fronts resolving in the same direction at once, on a timeline no one inside it appears to have planned for.

Key assumption: that Section 301's investigation-based authority is legally distinguishable from IEEPA's emergency-powers claim in a way the courts will honor — an assumption now facing its first real test rather than a settled one. The indicator worth watching is not the lawsuit's ultimate outcome, which will take months, but whether CIT grants expedited briefing or a temporary restraining order in the next two weeks, the same early signal that preceded February's injunction against the tariffs' predecessor.
