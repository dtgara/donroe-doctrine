Base case (50%): the Hormuz disruption persists through the October 27-28 FOMC meeting, Brent trades between $98 and $110, and the Federal Reserve holds on October 28 while signaling that a December increase remains available. Headline CPI of 3.7 percent was above the 3.6 percent consensus, and markets moved the probability of an October increase from about 20 percent to about 50 percent in a day.[^1] The disruption itself is not new; the change this week is that its price effect now appears in the inflation data the Fed uses.

Downside case (35%): the Fed raises on October 28, one week before the midterm elections. The 10-year yield touched 5.33 percent on the CPI release, and a further rate increase would reach households through mortgage and credit costs at the point in the electoral calendar when the President has said he is most attentive to them. Transit through the strait fell from about 20.8 million barrels on October 8 to 2.9 million on October 11, which would extend the inflation impulse.

Tail case (15%): Iran accepts a reopening arrangement before the election and Brent falls below $98. The administration has so far declined the terms offered, and the alternative it is now weighing, easing sanctions on tankers to reduce freight costs, treats the symptom without altering the supply position.

Key assumption: the Fed treats the energy-driven inflation as persistent enough to price but not yet persistent enough to answer with a rate increase before it sees the October data. The assumption fails if core CPI, which was 2.6 percent in September, rises in the October release, or if Brent settles above $110, the upper bound of the range most analysts have used since the strait closed.
