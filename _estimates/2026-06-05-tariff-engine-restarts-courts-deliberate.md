Base case (55%): tariff policy continues to expand this month through new Section 301 investigations and sector-specific proclamations even as the Section 122 global tariff's legal status remains unresolved, on the operating assessment that litigation timelines run slower than the policy calendar. The steel, aluminum, and copper tariff adjustment and the forced-labor tariff proposal now moving through Section 301 both proceed on schedules that assume the underlying 10 percent global tariff will still be standing when they take effect — an assumption the administration has not had to test yet, since the Court of International Trade's May ruling remains under appeal with no expedited hearing scheduled. Downside case (30%): an adverse appellate ruling forces a rapid unwind of the 10 percent global tariff on a timeline the administration does not control, producing the kind of sudden, court-ordered reversal the tariff regime has otherwise avoided by proceeding country by country and sector by sector, each action individually harder to challenge than the broad global tariff has proven to be. Tail case (15%): the rare earth and magnet export resumption negotiated in May is formally reversed by Beijing in response to an unrelated dispute, most plausibly the chip subsidiary licensing rule confirmed this week, restarting a restriction cycle barely three weeks into its pause.

Key assumption: that trade policy announced from the executive branch and trade policy validated by the courts are, for operational purposes, the same policy. They are not currently the same policy — one is a set of proclamations with immediate effect, the other a pending judicial question with an uncertain and possibly retroactive answer. The gap between them is being managed, for now, by proceeding as though it will close in the administration's favor, which spares anyone the trouble of building a contingency for the alternative before the court says so directly. The indicator that would change this assessment is a scheduling order from the appellate court; none has been issued as of this week.
