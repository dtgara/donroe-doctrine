Base case (55%): Brent trades between $105 and $120 through the October 27-28 FOMC meeting and the November 3 elections, with crude exports through Hormuz holding near pre-conflict levels and attacks on Gulf infrastructure continuing at the current rate. Brent settled at $113.17 on October 22, a 4 percent gain, after a strike on Aramco's Shaybah field and attacks near Dammam and Yanbu.[^1] The administration's response this week was domestic: an $8 billion program to offset farmers' diesel costs, announced at a rally in Kansas on October 19.[^2]

Downside case (35%): Iran expands the conflict. Tehran has said it may do so if what it calls illegal transits through Hormuz continue, and the IRGC said it struck four tankers transferring cargoes in the northern Persian Gulf, a claim the President has disputed.[^3] A sustained interruption would place Brent above $120 before the election and extend the diesel shortage the farm program is designed to cushion.

Tail case (10%): an arrangement reopening product shipments through the strait is reached before November 3, and Brent falls below $100. The administration has not signaled movement in that direction, and the President has tied decisions on Iran to the post-election period.

Key assumption: both sides defer a decisive escalation until after November 3. The assumption fails if the United States or Israel strikes Iran before the election, contrary to the timing reported by the New York Times, or if an attack on Saudi export infrastructure interrupts shipments rather than only causing casualties, as the Shaybah strike did not.
