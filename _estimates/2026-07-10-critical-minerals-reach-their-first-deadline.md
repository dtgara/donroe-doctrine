Base case (70%): the July 13 update on critical-minerals negotiations produces a narrow set of price-floor agreements and import restructuring measures, consistent with the pattern established by the January proclamation and the February Critical Minerals Ministerial — an incremental checkpoint rather than a comprehensive settlement, which is how every prior milestone in this file has resolved. The fifty-four-country scope of the Ministerial suggests the update is more likely to formalize existing bilateral arrangements than to announce anything genuinely new. Downside case (20%): negotiations stall, or produce measures broad enough to draw retaliation from allied processors who were partners rather than targets of the original strategy, complicating the Project Vault stockpile's stated purpose of derisking supply chains it now also directly finances — a tension between the stockpile's public rationale and its financing structure that the update is not designed to address and may inadvertently highlight. Tail case (10%): the ongoing press inquiry into family financial ties to at least fourteen mineral projects — including a tungsten arrangement in Kazakhstan — escalates from a reporting matter into a congressional one, a development assessed as unlikely given the doctrine's established resistance to this specific category of pressure, documented at length elsewhere in this archive.

Key assumption: that the distinction between derisking the critical-mineral supply chain and enriching the family financing its derisking continues to be treated, officially, as immaterial to the negotiations themselves. Should that assumption fail — through litigation, disclosure, or an unrelated political shift that makes the distinction newly convenient for someone to draw — the entire Project Vault financing structure would need to be reassessed, not merely the mining sector built on top of it. The indicator most worth watching is not the July 13 update's content, which is likely to be modest, but whether the update addresses the financing structure at all, or is drafted narrowly enough to avoid the question.
