Base case (55%): the Section 301 replacement holds through the fall without a successful court challenge, on the theory that an investigation-backed statute — unlike the emergency-powers claim the Supreme Court rejected in February — was built specifically to survive the scrutiny that felled its predecessor. Front-loaded cargo at the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach, which posted a record 2.47 million TEUs in July, unwinds through August roughly as the Port Tracker's own forecast anticipates, and the administration's "no-stacking" assurance on steel and aluminum holds as written.

Downside case (30%): the same jurisdictional ambiguity that produced six months of litigation under IEEPA reappears under Section 301, as importers test whether a tariff wall covering 99.4 percent of US trade, however statutorily sourced, still exceeds what Congress delegated — reopening the injunction risk the administration spent the spring litigating away. Concurrently, oil above $90 a barrel and a 10-year Treasury yield at an 18-month high harden the case for Chair Warsh's FOMC to hike on July 29 rather than hold, converting a trade story into a rates story within a single news cycle.

Tail case (15%): Canada's response to the new Section 338 tariffs — 50 percent on goods USMCA does not shield — escalates into retaliatory measures that draw in the broader North American supply chain the administration has otherwise left untouched this year, opening a fourth simultaneous front (China, Iran, Gulf shipping, now Canada) that tests the doctrine's improvisational capacity past what six months of practice have demonstrated it can absorb.

Key assumption: that Section 301's narrower, investigation-backed authority is durable in a way Section 122's balance-of-payments claim was not — an assumption resting on statutory text rather than on any court having yet ruled on it. The indicator worth watching is not the tariff rate itself but whether a legal filing appears in the next two weeks challenging the aggregate scope of the 60-country action, the same procedural question that took down its predecessor in February.
