Base case (55%): the standoff continues through the November 3 midterm elections without a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz or a US strike. On October 8 the President said Washington was having "productive discussions" with Iran and that no attack was planned before the midterms, following reports that one was under consideration.[^1] Iran's foreign minister said Tehran is reviewing the US response to its proposal to reopen the strait within seven days. Brent fell to roughly $103.5 on the statement.

Downside case (30%): the commitment on timing proves to be a commitment on sequencing. A stated restraint that expires on November 4 is also a stated option for November 4, and the market may price it as one well before then. Further attacks on crude shipments, which lifted Brent about 4 percent on October 8, would raise the cost of waiting for both sides.

Tail case (15%): talks produce a revised reopening arrangement before the election. This requires the administration to accept terms it declined on September 28, and the 100 million barrels the G7 agreed to release on October 2 reduces the pressure on it to do so.

Of the three cases, the first is the only one in which the date of the election is the operative variable. The President has now linked the timing of a military decision to an electoral calendar in public, which narrows the range of interpretations available to Tehran.

Key assumption: the administration continues to weigh the inflation cost of the blockade against its electoral cost on the same horizon. The assumption fails if Brent closes below $100 for several consecutive sessions, which would suggest the G7 release is working, or if either government restates terms for the strait publicly, which would suggest the talks have a content the statements have not described.

[^1]: Oil falls as Trump comments on Iran talks ease supply concerns, Reuters, October 9, 2026.
