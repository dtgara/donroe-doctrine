Base case (40%): the events of June 11 and June 12 are reconciled in the coming weeks as a single, if disorderly, negotiating sequence — declaration, escalation, and eventual settlement — consistent with the pattern this archive has previously described as escalation followed by declaration of victory, followed by the next escalation. Under this reading, the June 11 announcement was accurate as a statement of negotiating intent and simply overtaken, within a day, by an IAEA finding and an Israeli operation neither side fully controlled — a sequencing failure rather than a false statement. Downside case (35%): the IAEA's breach finding and the scale of the Israeli strikes on military leadership and nuclear scientists mark a genuine rupture rather than a temporary complication, and the June 11 announcement is remembered, if it is remembered at all, as the moment immediately preceding the war's second and more destructive phase. The House's vote earlier in the week to constrain the President's war powers suggests that at least part of Washington was already pricing in this outcome before the IAEA finding made it explicit. Tail case (25%): both readings are simultaneously true — the war ends in the specific sense the President described on June 11 and continues in the specific sense the IAEA and Israel are acting on days later — a description vague enough that the doctrine's supporters and its critics can each find it confirming, which is itself a data point about how the announcement was constructed.

Key assumption: that a declaration of the war's end is a data point about the war, rather than a data point about the timing of the declaration relative to whatever comes next. The confidence rating on this issue is Low because, for the first time in three issues, that assumption is genuinely and publicly in doubt — not merely doubted by this newsletter, but by the IAEA, the Israeli government, and a bipartisan majority of the House, within the same 48 hours the announcement was made.
